# Castcorder design

The agreed design as of 2026-11-10. Vocabulary is defined in [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md); the decisions with real trade-offs are in [adr/](./adr/). This file records everything else that was decided.

## Product

A free, open-source desktop app for recording podcasts, at `castcorder.app`. Participants talk over a live call while every machine records its own sources locally at full quality, then delivers the files to the host. It should feel like an application (install, copy link, talk), not a self-hosting project.

## Roles and access

- The host is a machine running the app. It serves all of its rooms silently whenever the app runs (one live call at a time; see "Rooms and calls"); someone at the machine joins explicitly to become a participant.
- Producers hold in-call controls: takes, the recording profile, selecting anyone's sources, password, lock, kick. Whoever is at the host is a producer. Only someone at the host grants or revokes the role, and only to a trusted device.
- Producers cannot delete tracks, change the storage location, delete the call, or appoint producers.
- No accounts. Every device generates its own key: per install for the app, per browser profile for the web. A device may be linked to a person (ADR 0005, "Persons and linked devices"): the person's devices are trusted producers in each other's rooms; a device never linked stays anonymous.
- A call link is the host key plus a call secret, carried in the URL fragment.
- Per room: producer-gated (default; guests wait until a producer joins) or always open. Optional password. Lock admits trusted devices only. Kick bans a device key. Regenerating the link fixes a leak.
- Joining discloses that a producer can see and select the guest's sources. A recording indicator is always visible.

## Recording

- Tracks are raw: no echo cancellation, noise suppression, gain, or resampling. Capture uses shared mode with OS effects bypassed, at the device's configured format.
- Audio is written as PCM/WAV to local disk on the highest-priority thread, then converted to FLAC after the take.
- Each participant has one live source per kind and a set of recorded sources. A producer can record several microphones per participant (for example a backup beside the main mic).
- Video recording is optional per call and independent of live video. Webcam only. Default is a real-time hardware encode at a visually lossless bitrate; camera-native passthrough and lossless are app-only profile options.
- A device that cannot meet the recording profile reports that to the producer. No automatic fallback in v1.
- The host writes each guest's live stream to disk as received, as a safety track.
- Host storage: `<library>/<room>/<call>/<take N>/` holding raw tracks, aligned tracks, safety tracks, and timing metadata. FLAC by default for raw and aligned tracks, configurable.

## Takes

- A producer starts a take: 3-second countdown, every device starts its recorded sources and acknowledges each one. A source that fails raises a warning and never blocks the take.
- Only a producer stops a take. It otherwise continues until every participant has left. The host has a configurable maximum take length (default 4 hours, may be off).
- A participant who joins or rejoins mid-take starts recording immediately.
- Before a take each device reports free disk space; the producer is warned when a device has under about an hour's worth. Recording is never stopped pre-emptively. Devices report free disk when joining, at each take start and every minute (a browser: storage quota less usage; the host: the library volume through the disk port); the host warns producers in an amber banner and per person in People when a device holds under an hour at the recording profile's data rate (audio 384 kB/s, plus 1 MB/s with video), and the take still starts.

## Never losing data

1. Capture always writes to local disk first. The network is never in the recording path.
2. A source dropout or crash ends a segment; the track continues in a new segment when the source returns.
3. The host keeps a manifest per take of expected tracks and verified received data.
4. Any reconnection from the same device resumes missing transfers automatically, even days later. A device in no call syncs over a sync-only connection (see "Background sync"): a guest that left, an app guest in the tray, a page reopened later.
5. If the host is lost mid-take, guests keep recording locally until it returns or they stop manually.
6. Local copies are deleted only after the host confirms verified receipt. App guests keep them 7 more days; browser guests delete on confirmation.

The outbox syncs whenever the host is connected and no take is recording: audio before video, oldest take first. Sync and live traffic compete, with priority hints where the platform honours them. Transfer is our own verified, resumable, range-based protocol over the existing connection, sized to suit S3 multipart later.

Browser guests store tracks in the browser's private filesystem: persistent storage is requested (best effort), and the guest is told to return in the same browser to finish syncing. Chromium browsers and Firefox are supported targets; Safari is a medium-low priority. Chromium records on the microphone's clock (MediaStreamTrackProcessor); other browsers use an AudioWorklet fallback, with any detected drift written to the track's metadata.

## Timing

Each device continuously measures its clock offset to the host and logs it beside the samples. The host renders aligned audio tracks (offset and drift corrected, with an offset-only option) as derived files. Timing metadata is a JSON sidecar, exported as Audacity labels and Audition marker CSV; aligned WAVs carry a broadcast-wave timestamp. Video is delivered raw with timing metadata and is not aligned.

## Architecture

- Star topology through the host (ADR 0001). About 4 participants with video, 6 with reduced or no video. Audio is prioritised over live video.
- Rust throughout. A headless core does capture, recording, forwarding, and sync: GStreamer for media, str0m for WebRTC transport and forwarding.
- One Leptos client (client-only mode) runs in the browser and in the Tauri desktop shell. It talks to a core through one interface with two implementations: the native core in the app, an in-browser WASM core for browser guests.
- One shared protocol crate of typed requests, responses, and events, used by every component.
- One app for hosts and app guests. It is a user-level process that closes to the tray, with optional start at login. A `castcorder://` handler opens call links.
- The core reaches time, network, disk, and capture devices only through port crates (ADR 0003).

## Services

- Browser client: a project-hosted static site, one path per release, pinned in each call link (ADR 0002).
- Rendezvous: a WebSocket mailbox. The host holds a connection while running; guests send connection info encrypted to the host key. Deployed as a Cloudflare Worker with one Durable Object per host key, serving the client too; the Rust rendezvous shares its mailbox logic and is the self-hosted and test implementation (ADR 0004).
- Rendezvous on Cloudflare (`castcorder/worker`, workers-rs; ADR 0004). The mailbox state machine (routing, per-IP and per-host-key limits, whether relay credentials may be minted) is the I/O-free `castcorder-mailbox` crate; the Rust rendezvous wraps it in axum, the Worker in Durable Objects. Every `/ws` connection names the host whose mailbox it is for (`?mailbox=HOST_ID`; a guest joining a call that sends only `?host=` is routed by that), since a WebSocket cannot move to a Durable Object after the first frame: the host its own key, a guest, a status query or a link request the host it addresses. A scoped connection may only register as, or offer to, that host, in both implementations. The Worker refuses `/ws` naming no valid host id before touching any Durable Object, limits connections per IP at the edge (the `ratelimits` binding), and hands the upgrade to that host key's Durable Object, which holds the host's and its guests' WebSockets with the hibernation API: each connection's mailbox state rides in its attachment and its connection id in its tag, and a woken object restores them (rate-limit buckets start full again, and connection ids restart from the clock so a late answer never reaches a newer guest). The object sends no pings, so the host pings after 20 s of silence; the runtime answers without waking the object. Static assets serve `/v/<version>/`. The hello carries `stun_port: null` and the `STUN_URLS` var (`stun:stun.cloudflare.com:3478`); a host without a STUN port takes the first `stun:` URL of the hello's `ice_servers` to learn its server-reflexive address. Relay credentials (`worker/src/relay.rs`): `RELAY=cloudflare` mints Cloudflare Realtime TURN credentials (`generate-ice-servers`, secrets `CF_TURN_KEY_ID` and `CF_TURN_API_TOKEN`; every TURN URL it returns except port 53, so TCP and TLS on 443 too); `RELAY=coturn` mints coturn REST credentials (`TURN_URLS`, secret `TURN_SECRET`; the scheme shared with the Rust rendezvous in `castcorder-mailbox`), which CI exercises; `TURN_TTL` (default a day) sets their lifetime, and each `IceServer` carries `expires` since Cloudflare's usernames are opaque (the host renews by it, and so does a browser guest: at 80% of their life its call or sync-only connection takes a fresh hello naming the host, applies its ICE servers with `setConfiguration` and sends `{"type":"restart"}` on the control channel, and the host answers with an ICE-restart offer, so the guest gathers a relay candidate with the new credentials without reconnecting). Each host key may mint `RELAY_DAILY_CAP` credentials per UTC day (default 300), counted in its Durable Object's storage so hibernation and restarts do not reset it; past that, `relay_quota`. A relay that cannot mint answers `relay_unavailable`. Locally `task worker:dev` runs it under `wrangler dev` with no account. Tested: the mailbox crate's unit tests (scope, restore through JSON, relay decisions, coturn REST and Cloudflare answers, the daily cap); `task test:system:worker` runs the tier 3 system test against the Worker under `wrangler dev` (in CI), with the host idle 50 s so its Durable Object hibernates and must restore it, sync-only connections, link requests (a browser profile and a second install), the rejoin scenario (status queries), and with Docker a `--relay-only` host and a forced-relay guest through coturn on credentials the Worker minted with a 30 s `TURN_TTL`, which the guest renews before they expire and keeps its call; `task test:worker:relay` checks the credentials and the daily cap across a `wrangler dev` restart, and Cloudflare Realtime TURN for real when `CF_TURN_KEY_ID`/`CF_TURN_API_TOKEN` are set (skipped otherwise, also in CI until the deploy secrets exist).
- Relay: Cloudflare Realtime TURN when deployed, stock coturn when self-hosted and in CI, with short-lived credentials minted by the rendezvous per host key. STUN: `stun.cloudflare.com`.
- Both are rate-limited per host key and per IP address. Charging may attach to the host key later.
- Relay metering. Every relay credential is named `<expiry>:<host id>`, for the host and for its guests, so coturn's Prometheus exporter (`prometheus`, `prometheus-username-labels`) reports relayed bytes per host key. The rendezvous scrapes it (`--turn-metrics`), adds the growth of each counter to the host key's day (a new or reset counter counts in full) and keeps a 30-day ledger in one JSON file in `--data-dir`, written atomically so a restart neither forgets nor re-counts. coturn counts a session when it ends (deallocated or expired), so usage lags a live allocation by up to the credential TTL. A host key at or over `--relay-budget` (bytes per rolling 30 days) gets no relay credentials: `registered` and the guest's hello carry `relay_error: relay_quota: ...`, the host logs it and asks again in 15 minutes, guests log it, and direct connections keep working. coturn's `user-quota` and `max-bps` cap allocations per credential and bandwidth per session (coturn counts them per username, not per host key). Operators read usage per host key with `GET /usage` on the loopback-only `--admin` listener or `castcorder-rendezvous usage --data-dir DIR`. Tested: ledger and budget unit tests; a seeded ledger refuses a real host and guest in `task check`; in the relay tier a relayed call's bytes reach the ledger through real coturn, and the rendezvous restarted with a 1-byte budget refuses with `relay_quota`.
- Structured logs in every component, correlated by call, device and take, kept locally in rotating files. For now, guests' logs sync automatically to the host as files through the outbox, disclosed at join, and land beside the call's takes. No third-party destination.

## Repository and delivery

```
/Taskfile.yml           includes each service's taskfile
/docker-compose.yml     local dev: rendezvous + coturn
/CONTEXT.md, /docs/
/castcorder/            our code: one Cargo workspace, crates prefixed castcorder-
/castcorder/worker/     the Cloudflare Worker (its own workspace, wasm32; AGPL)
/coturn/                relay configuration
/site/                  static deploy config
/deploy/                infrastructure (deploy/README.md: running the Worker locally)
```

- Licences: MIT/Apache-2.0 for the app and client crates, AGPL for the rendezvous, the shared mailbox crate and the Worker. No GPL GStreamer plugins.
- Tooling mirrors the genesys repo: Taskfile as the single entry point, the `.githooks` chain running beads then pre-commit, hygiene-only hooks, beads for issues.
- CI: GitHub-hosted runners (the repo is public). Linux runs every automated test tier on each pull request. A release is a `vX.Y.Z` tag on `main` that builds Windows, macOS, and Linux installers into a draft GitHub Release; publishing it serves the matching web client at `/v/X.Y.Z/`. One version in `castcorder/Cargo.toml`, a Keep a Changelog `CHANGELOG.md`, and wire encodings frozen per release ([docs/release.md](release.md), ADR 0006).
- Builds are unsigned. Windows is first-class, macOS best-effort, Linux gets builds. Tauri's updater uses its own key: release.yml signs updater artifacts and attaches `latest.json` once the key is in the repository's secrets; the app's update check is not built yet.
- English-only UI in v1.

## M0 results

All five spikes returned "go with caveats". Proof code and full findings are in `castcorder/spikes/<name>/FINDINGS.md`. What they settled:

- **Transport: str0m.** Three Chromium guests exchanged forwarded audio and video through a str0m host, with a verified data-channel transfer. str0m does ICE only, so the host needs its own STUN and TURN client. Leaves are detected by an application heartbeat (about 1 s), not ICE (14-22 s). One video codec per call (VP8). Per-guest video step-down means guests publish two simulcast layers and the host picks one or drops video.
- **Media engine: GStreamer.** One capture teed to a byte-identical WAV and a processed Opus branch; a stalled live branch does not affect the recording. We keep our own WAV writer (header rewritten each second, RF64 past 4 GiB, repair on startup). Video records to Matroska, which survived a hard kill intact. Device loss is detected by the device monitor plus a no-data watchdog.
- **Transfer: bao-tree.** Verified, resumable transfer natively and in browser WASM. 16 KiB chunk groups, 1 MiB units, 64 KiB frames, 8 MiB S3 parts. The hash is computed while recording. Browser storage writes must be batched per unit.
- **Browser recorder: Rust and Leptos work.** Raw and processed opens of one microphone coexist in Chromium; the raw one is lossless. Recording is a float32 WAV patched in place, recoverable after a browser kill. One 68-line JS file is unavoidable.
- **Protocol: the typed protocol crate.** Leptos server functions do run over a data channel but fit the two-core design and server-pushed events poorly.

What they changed or left open:

- **Browser capture clock.** An AudioWorklet runs on the output device's clock, and a 150 s soak showed Chrome inserting or dropping 10 ms blocks as it drifts from the microphone. This must be fixed before M1, likely by capturing on the microphone's clock in Chromium, and any insert or drop must be recorded in timing metadata.
- **Start-up gap.** A freshly opened virtual microphone delivered nothing for about 2 s. Acceptable if not easy to avoid: producers wait for everyone to be back before talking.
- **Unverified:** Firefox and Safari end to end (medium-low priority), any relayed or lossy network path, Windows and macOS capture, and virtual webcams on CI runners.

## M4 live video (part 1: live only)

- **Sending.** A browser guest opens its camera with its own `getUserMedia` (a missing or refused camera never costs the microphone) and sends it on one video transceiver created at connect with two VP8 simulcast encodings: `h` full size up to 800 kbit/s, `l` half size up to 150 kbit/s. Turning the camera off or on swaps the sender's track (`replaceTrack`, null when off) and releases or reopens the device, so it never renegotiates. It also says so (`CameraState` on "control", sent after every join and on each change; the host keeps the latest per participant, relays it, and replays it to latecomers), and the others' tile for it shows the camera-off avatar while it is off, whatever the browser reports of the silent track (a track unmuting does not clear it while it is signalled off). Headless Chromium and Firefox both send both layers.
- **Forwarding.** Per receiver, every 250 ms, the host reserves the forwarded audio's measured rate, then gives every video track its lowest live layer while it fits 90 % of the receiver's TWCC estimate, then upgrades one step at a time (stepping up needs 25 % to spare and must stay admitted for 3 s, except a source's first layer; stepping down is immediate); otherwise that receiver gets no video. Layer rates are smoothed (a quarter of each 500 ms window's rate) from a nominal prior per rid (l 100, h 800 kbit/s), so video starts at once and an encoder's swings do not flip layers. A layer silent for 2 s counts as not sent. A new layer is asked for a keyframe (PLI per rid: a simulcast track has no stream without one) and takes over at that keyframe, with RTP times shifted to continue the outgoing stream. The estimator is told to sustain what the next upgrade needs and probe for the best layers. str0m sends audio unpaced, ahead of queued video.
- **UI.** Each remote participant gets a tile (video plus audio) labelled with the participant's display name (the host sends everyone the participant list; the stream id `<name>-<participant id>` keys it); the guest's own camera shows in a muted preview tile. The host's native participant stays audio only.
- **Tested.** A simulation through str0m's real estimator on `SimNetwork` with capacity limits: an unlimited receiver climbs to `h`, one at 500 kbit/s gets `l`, one at 120 kbit/s gets no video, and all keep every audio frame. The browser test decodes each other's video in Chromium and Firefox, sees two layers sent, and toggles a camera without an offer. A host started with `--bandwidth-cap 250` (a configured cap on the estimate the allocation uses, not network shaping) makes real Chromium and Firefox guests settle on `l` (320x180) with audio flowing.
- **Open.** Native camera capture; Safari; real lossy and congested paths; CPU cost of two encodes on weak machines.

## M4 video recording (part 2: browser guests)

- **Profile.** Whether a call records video is a room setting (`RoomAction::SetRecordVideo`, the producer's "Record video" button, `castcorder-host --record-video true|false`), saved in `room.json`, default off. A take carries it in its status (`TakeStatus::Recording { take, video }`), so a change applies from the next take and mid-take joiners get it too.
- **Recording.** A browser guest whose camera is on records the camera track itself with MediaRecorder: WebM, VP9 where supported (Chromium) else VP8 (Firefox), at 0.3 bit per pixel per frame, 4 to 60 Mbit/s. Every second a chunk (a `Blob`) goes to the storage worker, which reads it with `FileReaderSync` and appends it to `tracks/<take>-<ms>-cam.webm` with one OPFS write and flush, rewriting a JSON sidecar. A camera turned off mid-take ends that video track; turned on again, a new one starts. A crash leaves a valid WebM prefix (unknown-size elements), finalised as `recovered` at the next scan. Kept as WebM on the host (it is Matroska; `ffmpeg -c copy` adds duration and cues for editors).
- **Resolution.** The recorded track is the camera as opened for the live call (640x360 ideal); raising the capture size needs `scaleResolutionDownBy` on both simulcast layers first, or the `l` layer and the bandwidth-cap test change. Open.
- **Timing.** `<track>.timing.jsonl` with the audio header (`sample_rate` 0), the clock exchanges, `video_start` (device and host time, codec, size, bitrate), one `chunk` per write (offset, bytes, device and host time, the recorder's timecode) and `video_end`. Video is not aligned.
- **Sync.** The webm, sidecar and timing file are `SourceKind::Video` outbox items; the guest offers in sync order and the host pulls every audio item first. On the host: `<call>/takeNN/takeNN_<who>_cam.webm` (`_<who>-2_cam` for a second camera run) beside `.json` and `.timing.jsonl`. Only audio completions trigger alignment. A 48 MiB video syncs with under 8 MiB of heap growth (`castcorder-core/tests/video_memory.rs`).
- **Cannot meet profile.** A browser whose MediaRecorder supports neither codec sends `Request::ProfileUnmet` once per take; every producer gets `Event::ProfileUnmet` and sees it in the panel. The take, and that guest's audio, go on.
- **Tested.** Take 3 of the browser test records video in Chromium and Firefox: each webm has an EBML header and DocType webm, ffprobe decodes VP9 (Chromium) or VP8 (Firefox) of the expected length, the timing chunks tile the file and the recorder starts within tens of ms of the take start; per device the host verified all audio before any video; A's audio stays bit-exact in Chromium. In Chromium guest B's `isTypeSupported` is replaced to say no, and the producer sees B's warning while B's audio syncs. Takes 1 and 2 (default off) have no video.
- **Open.** In Chromium, reopening the camera mid-take stalled the page's raw audio capture once by 40 ms (seen with fake devices; the Firefox run toggles the camera instead); capture resolution above the live request; Safari.

## M5 desktop shell (part 1)

- **Host as a library.** `castcorder-host` is a library plus the unchanged binary: `castcorder_host::start(Config, on_link)` runs a host in-process and `Running::stop` finishes the host microphone take and the safety tracks, then ends the listeners, the rendezvous connection and the call thread so the ports are free. Both the binary and the app parse the same host flags (`Config::apply_args`).
- **App.** `castcorder-app` (Tauri 2, feature `desktop`; `task app`) starts the host with the app, and its window shows the producer page (`http://localhost:<port>/`), where the person at the host joins explicitly as in a browser. Closing the window hides it to the tray and the host keeps serving; tray Quit finishes and stops it. "Start at login" is a tray toggle (tauri-plugin-autostart, launches `--hidden`), off by default. A host that cannot start (a port in use) shows the reason in the window.
- **Links.** `castcorder://<rendezvous>/<path>#<host-id>.<call-secret>` opens the matching `https://` call link in a call window, after the fragment validates as a call link (tolerating `%23` and trailing slashes). The app registers the scheme per user at start (`--no-register` skips it) until installers do; tauri-plugin-single-instance hands a second launch's link to the running app instead of starting a second host.
- **Tested.** In `task check`: the app's host control starts the host on OS-picked loopback ports, `GET /role` answers, a str0m guest signs its offer for that host id and connects over UDP, then the host stops and starts again on the same ports; the link mapping and flag parsing are unit tests. `task app:check` (CI job `app`, under xvfb on Linux) adds clippy for the shell and `castcorder-app --smoke`: the hidden window loads the producer page, `APP_READY`, clean stop.
- **Installers.** `task app:bundle` (and `.github/workflows/release.yml`, on a `v*` tag or workflow_dispatch) runs `cargo tauri build --config tauri.bundle.conf.json`: NSIS and MSI on Windows, `.app` and `.dmg` on macOS, `.deb` and AppImage on Linux, all unsigned. The trunk-built client ships as the `client` resource and the in-process host serves it from there (beside the exe, `../lib/Castcorder/`, or `../Resources/`). Every release build has `native-capture`. The Windows installers bundle a trimmed, LGPL-only GStreamer (`task app:gst:stage`, `scripts/gst_bundle.py`: the 11 plugins capture, live and playback use and the DLLs they import, with their licences and a source notice; about 12 MB unpacked): the libraries beside the exe, the plugins in `gstreamer-1.0/`, which castcorder-capture-gst scans at init. The Windows installers also ship the MSVC runtime app-locally (a clean machine lacks it; `scripts/check_install_dlls.py` fails the smoke on any imported DLL that is neither in the install nor a system DLL, since the hosted runner has the runtime in System32). The macOS build is one universal (x86_64 and arm64) `.app` with the same trimmed set from the official universal GStreamer.framework (about 34 MB, both slices): the libraries in `Contents/Frameworks`, the executable's rpath (`build.rs`), and the plugins in `Contents/Frameworks/gstreamer-1.0`, copied unmodified (every framework library is linked as `@rpath/<name>`); `Info.plist` adds the microphone and camera usage descriptions macOS requires. The `.deb` (built on Ubuntu 24.04 for GStreamer 1.24, so glibc 2.39 and newer) depends on the distro's GStreamer packages. The AppImage carries the GStreamer libraries the executable links (the build machine's 1.24) but no plugins, and AppImage tooling patches the built-in plugin path out of its libgstreamer, so castcorder-capture-gst scans the distro's plugin directories when no plugin was found: native capture there needs the system's base, good and PulseAudio plugins at 1.24 or older (GStreamer rejects plugins newer than its core), so on a distro with newer GStreamer the AppImage has the webview microphone only and the `.deb` is the supported route. `castcorder-app --smoke-gst` checks that GStreamer initialises and finds every element; the Windows smoke runs it with no other GStreamer on PATH and the macOS smoke with the build's GStreamer.framework moved away (`scripts/check_app_dylibs.py` also checks every library is the system's or in the `.app`, in both slices), and both check every plugin loads from the install. A tag creates a draft GitHub Release with the installers; the smoke run covers the installed app on Windows, macOS and Linux (the `.deb`, and `--smoke-gst` from the AppImage on a runner with the system GStreamer).
- **Native microphone at runtime.** In a build with `native-capture`, the person at the host joins and leaves the call with an input GStreamer's device monitor lists, from the producer page ("Microphone on this machine") or through `HostControl::join_native`/`leave_native`; `--participate` is the same join at start. The page uses `GET /native`, `POST /native/join` and `POST /native/leave`, answered only on the localhost listener with a loopback `Host` and an `X-Castcorder-Native` header (a cross-site page cannot send it without a preflight). Joining mid-take records from the join; leaving mid-take finishes that take's microphone files first and closes the input. Tested in `task test:gst`: a WAV file through the capture port joins at runtime twice and each take's raw track is bit-exact.
- **Tray status.** The tray shows a one-line status (`2 guests, recording take 3, syncing 1`) as a disabled menu item, in the tooltip, as a `REC <take>` title (macOS) and as an icon with a red dot while a take records. The app polls `HostControl::live_status` once a second (a `Command::Live` to the call thread: guests, recording take, last take, guests with an outbox still syncing) and touches the OS only when it changed. "Copy call link" puts the current link on the clipboard (tauri-plugin-clipboard-manager); "Regenerate call link" rotates the call secret from the host machine (`Running::regenerate_link`, the same rotation as the producer action: guests in the call stay, the old link stops admitting) and copies the new link. Both need a rendezvous (a call link). Tested in `task check` through host control against a real host (guest joins: 1 guest; take starts: recording) and in `task app:check` (`--smoke` asserts the snapshot answers).
- **Open.** App guests use the browser client in the call window rather than the native core; updater; signed and notarized macOS builds.

## Rooms and calls

- **Layout.** The library root holds the host key (`host-identity.key`), the LAN certificate (`host-cert.pem`, `host-key.pem`) and the person (`person.json`, see "Persons and linked devices"). `<library>/<room>/` holds `room.json` (settings, trusted devices, producers, bans) and `call-secret` (the room's call link), and one folder per call, `<YYYY-MM-DD>[-N]` (UTC start date) with `call.json` (started, last active), its `takeNN/` and guests' `logs/`. The host's own log is in `<library>/logs/`. A folder from before rooms (`takeNN/` beside `room.json`) becomes a room with that one call at start: its takes and logs move into a dated call folder, idempotently (a move cut short finishes next start).
- **One host, many rooms, one live call.** The rendezvous offer's secret picks the room; LAN pages name it with `?room=` (none: the live room, else the default room, `--room`; `--call` is its old name). A call is live while anyone is in it or a take records. An offer for another room gets `waiting:` and the page asks again, so a guest on a room that is not live waits in the lobby. With nothing live, the first one the room admits opens it: in a producer-gated room a producer or the host machine, in an open room anyone.
- **Routing.** The first one in opens the room's most recently active call if it was active less than the idle gap ago (`--idle-gap` minutes, default 120), else a new call. The open call is marked active every minute while live and when it empties, so a rejoin, even after a host restart, lands in the same folder and take numbers continue. Routing reads "now" through the clock port (`Config::clock`).
- **Choosing a call.** In a call, producers see the room's calls (`Event::Calls`) and can move everyone into a new call or a past one (`RoomAction::NewCall`, `ContinueCall`), only while no take records and every device has synced. Before joining, a remote producer who was in a past call continues it by naming it in its offer (see "Rejoining"). The host machine's home does it before joining: a past call is marked the most recently active, a new one is made, and the next join lands there.
- **Per-call tracks, one outbox.** Each device is told its call (`Event::Call`) after Joined and on every move, and keeps its tracks (and log files: the worker starts a new one when the call changes) under that call's scope (`<host>/<room>/<call>`). Whenever it is connected to any call of that host, its outbox offers every pending item of that host: this call's and those from before rooms with `OfferTrack`, another call's with `OfferCallTrack` naming its room and call. Order as always: audio, video, logs; oldest call, then oldest take first. The host records every device it tells a call in that call's `devices.jsonl` and files an `OfferCallTrack` into that call's folder only if the connection's verified device is listed there (or, for a call from before the list, named in its manifests); otherwise it refuses and the item stays on the device. The check reads only that call's folder, never which call or room the device is in now, so a later sync-only connection can use it as is.
- **Home.** The host machine's own page with no query (the desktop window) lists every room with its call link (Copy, New link), Start new call, Join (the room's lobby, `/?room=NAME`, with this page's microphone or the machine's native one, which mutes call-only) and its past calls with date, takes, Open folder and Continue this call. It uses `GET /rooms`, `POST /rooms`, `/rooms/link`, `/rooms/call`, `/folder/open` and `/native/mute`, answered only on the localhost listener with the native header.
- **Tested.** Unit: routing (recent within the gap, beyond it, explicit choice), call ids, migration and a migration cut short. `castcorder-app/tests/rooms.rs`: two rooms, the second waits while the first is live, a rejoin keeps the call folder and take numbers, a join after the simulated gap makes a new call, the other room then opens its own. Browser (`lib/rooms.mjs`, Chromium and Firefox): the home makes a room and copies its link, a guest on it waits, the producer joins from the home, leave and rejoin keep the call, a new call gets a new folder, the home lists both and Continue picks the old one; a guest that left mid-take joins a later call and its take lands in the old call's folder and leaves OPFS, while a track it claims for a call it was never in is refused and kept. Core (`cross_call_sim.rs`): tracks recorded in an old call file into that call verified and leave the device, audio first and oldest call first; a call the device was never in refuses its track; the device list survives a restart and a torn append. gst: muting the native microphone silences the encoder input while the recording stays bit-exact.
- **Open.** A take of another call completed this way (or over a sync-only connection) is not aligned automatically yet.

## Background sync

A device syncs whenever it and the host are both online, not only while it is in a call, so a trusted guest can leave at will and its files follow as bandwidth allows.

- **Sync-only connection.** The same WebRTC wire contract with the two data channels alone: no audio or video m-line (an offer with one is refused, `Host::accept_sync`), never sent anyone's media, so never renegotiated with. The offer says so (`sync_only` in the sealed offer, `"sync":true` on a LAN `/offer`) and is signed by the device key like any offer. It sends no Join and is not a participant: it neither opens, holds nor waits for a call and is never counted as anyone in one (the tray's guests, "live", the one-live-call rule), though the tray counts it as syncing.
- **Admission.** By device key alone, through any room's link (or `?room=`): no lock, password or producer gate applies, since it joins no call. A device kicked from that room is refused (`kicked:`; `Room::admit_sync`, read from that room's saved state when another room is loaded). It is accepted beside the live call of any room.
- **Authorization.** Its only request is `OfferCallTrack`; anything else is refused. Each item is checked against the call it names exactly as for a connection in a call (`call_library`: the device is in that call's `devices.jsonl` or its manifests) and refused if the device was kicked from that call's room; a refused item stays on the device. Items from before rooms name no call and wait for a join.
- **One library per folder.** Items file into their call's folder under the key `<room>/<call>`: the open call's library when it is that call (`CallSet::open_key`), else one kept for it. When a call opens or closes while sync-only connections exist, the library moves between the two slots, so one folder never has two manifest writers.
- **Below live media.** A sync-only connection asks for nothing new while a take records or a guest in the call is still syncing its own outbox; data already asked for still lands. Between connections there is no finer priority: the live call's media is on its own peer connections.
- **Browser guest.** After Leave, and when a page opens without joining (the host's own home excepted), the page lists the outbox for this host; with anything deliverable it connects sync-only (no microphone or camera) and offers it, retrying with the usual backoff while the page is open. The lobby's `#sync-state` shows the files left and then the outcome (all delivered, some refused and kept, or the device refused). Once nothing deliverable is left the connection closes; joining stops it. Test hook: `window.__castcorderHoldSync` holds it unconnected.
- **App.** Closing a call window leaves the call (its Leave button) and hides the window: its page goes on syncing from the tray. The last `castcorder://` link opened is remembered in the app's data folder (`last-call-link`) and reopened in a hidden call window at every start, at login too, so its page finishes the sync without joining.
- **Tested.** Transport sim: a sync-only guest beside a call gets no media and no renegotiation, its data channel round-trips, a media offer is refused. Core sim (`cross_call_sim.rs`): a sync-only puller files by key with no call open and carries on when that call opens midway. Room unit: lock, password and gate do not apply; a banned device and a bad id are refused. App (`tests/sync_only.rs`, real host): a guest records, leaves, another room's call goes live, the guest's sync-only connection is admitted beside it (not counted in it) and its take lands verified and bit-exact; other requests and a call it was never in are refused; once banned its items are refused. Browser (`lib/rooms.mjs`, Chromium and Firefox): a guest leaves mid-take and its page shows the take waiting; once another room is live the take lands over a sync-only connection and leaves OPFS while the foreign track is kept and reported; a guest kicked mid-take is refused sync-only and keeps its take.

## Rejoining

Anyone who has been in a call, and was not kicked, sees the rooms and calls they took part in, with live status, and can rejoin them (cast-zr8).

- **The list.** Each device keeps it itself, so it shows with the host offline: a browser profile in localStorage (`castcorder.rooms`, per origin), remembered whenever the host tells it its call (`Event::Call`): the room's page (the call link, or a LAN page with `?room=`), its host, and each call it was in. The lobby shows it as "Rooms you joined": a card per room (`#my-rooms li[data-room]`) with a status chip, who is in the call, Rejoin, and its calls. The host machine's own page remembers nothing (its home lists every room). The app's list is its call window's (the browser profile that holds its device key): the home links `castcorder://rooms`, which shows the call window, or reopens the last call link in it, whose lobby lists them; with no call link remembered there is nothing to list.
- **Status.** A status query is a sealed offer marked `status` (castcorder-link `OfferPlain::status`; `"status":true` on a LAN `/offer`) whose proof signs a fresh random nonce instead of an SDP. Like a sync-only connection it is admitted by the device key alone, but it opens no connection: it never opens, holds, waits for or counts toward a call and never loads a room (other rooms are read as saved). It needs no call secret, so a regenerated link does not lose the room: the host answers (`AnswerPlain::Status`, `HostStatus`) for every room the device was in (`Room::membership`, the old id's record for an upgraded device): live, busy (another room is live), the call a join lands in (the live call, else what routing would pick, else none), who is in the live call by name, whether the device is a producer of the room, the room's call link if the device is trusted, and only the calls it was in (each call's `devices.jsonl`, read without opening that call's library). A device the room never saw is told nothing; a kicked one only that it was removed. The lobby asks each host once when it shows, after Leave and every 20 s while visible; a host the rendezvous cannot reach shows "Host offline".
- **Rejoin.** Rejoin opens the room's page (or joins at once on that very page) and lands in its current call; normal join rules apply (a gated room waits for a producer, a locked one needs trust, the password). A past call is rejoined by naming it in the offer (`OfferPlain::call`, `"call"` on `/offer`; the page's `?call=`): after the usual admission it lands there if it is the room's current call; else a producer of the room who was in that call continues it (the host machine counts as one) while the room is not live, and in a live room lands in the live call, whose in-call Continue then applies (no take recording, everyone synced); anyone else is refused `not_current:` and their next Join takes the current call. The row offers Rejoin this call on the current call, Continue this call to a producer while the room is not live, else says a producer can continue it.
- **Removed.** A `kicked:` refusal (joining or syncing) marks the room removed on the device: it stays listed, says Removed, and offers no way back.
- **Tested.** Unit: membership (member, removed, producer, an upgraded device's old record); the wire shapes; the rendezvous passes a status query with a stale secret to the host. App (`tests/rejoin.rs`, real host): status for an unknown device, while in the call and after, who is in, the current call after the home starts a new one, only the calls the device was in, a call that does not exist refused `not_current:`, the host machine continuing a past call that others then join, a removed device, and a busy room. Core (`cross_call_sim.rs`): the device list read without the library, a torn tail ignored. Browser (`lib/rejoin.mjs`, Chromium, through a rendezvous of its own): a guest that left lists the room live with who is in it, across a reload; Rejoin and Rejoin this call land in the current call; a past call that is not current is refused for a guest and the next Join takes the current call; once granted the producer role it continues the past call; a kicked guest's room says Removed with no rejoin; with the host stopped, a reloaded page lists the room as offline. `castcorder-app` deeplink: `castcorder://rooms`.
- **Open.** A guest that is not trusted keeps only the link it joined with: after the link is regenerated its room shows Status unknown. A call from before the device list is not listed by status (its manifests are not scanned).

## Persons and linked devices

A person owns devices through a person key (ADR 0005, cast-40e.1). Contacts, presence, contact links, invites and revocation build on it later (cast-40e.2 to .6).

- **Person key.** Every app install makes a person on first start (`<library>/person.json`: the person key's Ed25519 secret, the person's display name, this device's name, this install's certificate and the devices linked from here; castcorder-host `person`). The person id is `p` and 40 hex digits of the key's SHA-256, never mistaken for a device id (`k...`). Losing every app install of a person loses the person.
- **Certificate.** The person key signs a device key: a domain tag, the person key, the device key, the kind (app or browser) and when (castcorder-link `person::DeviceCert`); names are not signed, so renaming needs nothing new. An app install's certificate is for its host key; a browser profile's for its WebCrypto key.
- **Linking.** On any app install of the person, "Link a device" (the home's "You" card, `POST /person/code`) makes a one-time link, single use for 10 minutes: `https://<rendezvous>/v/<version>/#link.<host-id>.<secret>` through the rendezvous once registered, and `https://<lan-ip>:<port>/#link.<host-id>.<secret>` to each LAN page. The new device sends a link request (`OfferPlain::link`, its proof signing a fresh nonce) sealed to that host key, through the rendezvous mailbox or the LAN page's `POST /link`. The first device to use the code claims it, bound to its key, and asks again every second or so until someone at the approving device approves or declines it, by its name and fingerprint (8 hex digits of its device id, shown on both). Approved, the next request gets `AnswerPlain::Link(Granted)`: the certificate, the person's name, and for an app install the person key (only ever inside the sealed answer, never from an HTTP endpoint).
- **Why these paths.** A link code works like a call link: the host key in it authenticates the answering device and the seal hides the contents from the rendezvous, so linking needs no new service, connection type or trust anchor. The LAN path lets two installs on one network link without a rendezvous; its TLS certificate is not checked, since the seal does that job.
- **An app install.** Pasting a link into the home ("Link this app", `POST /person/join`) links it in the background; once approved it replaces its own person with the one it joined (only while it has linked no device itself, which would be orphaned). Its rooms then belong to that person.
- **A browser profile.** Opening a link shows "Link this browser": the request goes through the page's own origin (the rendezvous mailbox for a `/v/` page, else the LAN host's `/link`). It keeps only the certificate and the person's name (localStorage `castcorder.person`), never the person key: its own key is a non-extractable WebCrypto key, and a person key kept as bytes in page storage would be the weakest copy. So only app installs link more devices.
- **Rooms belong to the person.** Every offer, status query and sync-only connection of a linked device carries its certificate (`cert` in the sealed offer or the LAN `/offer` JSON). A host whose own person it names, for the very key that signed the offer's proof, treats the device as the owner's (castcorder-host `room`, `Seat::owner`): it always enters, like the host machine (no password, lock, producer gate or ban; revocation is cast-40e.6), it is trusted and a producer, and a status query lists every room as one it may rejoin as a producer. It is not the host machine, so it cannot appoint producers. Owner is decided per offer and never saved in `room.json`.
- **Tested.** Unit: certificates (bound to device, kind, time and person; JSON shape), link codes (round trip, not a call link), the person store (made once, survives a restart, a copied library makes a new person), linking (pending until approved, only the claimant, declined, expired, forged, a browser never gets the key, an app adopts the person), and owner seats in the room. `castcorder-app/tests/person.rs`, two app installs with their own libraries: B links to A over A's LAN listener with A's approval and holds A's person after a restart; its own key offers to A's producer-gated, empty room through A's LAN listener: without the certificate (or with one for another key) it waits, with it it enters and starts a take as a producer.
- **Open.** The app's call window is a browser profile of its own: until it is linked (open a link from the app's home in it), the app joins other hosts' rooms as an anonymous device; linking it automatically is cast-40e.7. The devices linked from one install are listed only there, and there is no unlink yet (cast-40e.6). The linking UI has no browser e2e yet (cast-40e.8).

**UI layout v2 (planned).** The second pass (cast-d2o, 2026-11-24) is the "Castcorder UX 2: home, call, contacts" canvas (https://claude.ai/artifact/QRpduNS1CvaoLSEj7rQVBe); it keeps the tokens and the rules below and reorganises where things live. Implementation is epic cast-n56, with the contacts screens under cast-40e.

Each room shows one primary action (Join), a copy-link icon and a ... menu (start a fresh call, invite to a call now, people and settings, open the recordings folder, replace the link); past calls fold under a disclosure; rooms joined on other hosts sit below with live status.

- **Contacts.** Requests, Online and Offline groups with presence, Invite per contact, a detail pane (nickname, rooms you share, recordings on the way), Add contact by contact link. - **Lobby and leaving.** Camera preview and a mic meter before joining; the password field only when the room has one; a name is required. Leave lands on a "You left" page with delivery progress, Rejoin and Your calls.

- **Call.** One-row top bar with a fixed REC slot and Invite, People, Recordings, more. The People panel uses Host, Producer and Guest chips and icons (recording, space left, connection), a per-person menu (add as contact, make producer, their devices, remove) and folds room settings, calls and connection details. A phone gets a labelled dock with Leave.

- **Layout.** A lobby before joining (name, password, microphone, camera and speakers pickers, camera choice, the recording notice). In the call: a top bar with the recording state, the stage (one tile per participant, own tile first), one optional side panel (People and call controls, Recordings, Transcript) and a bottom dock (Mute, Camera, Hide me, Devices, Start/Stop take for producers, Leave). The conversation fills the window; producers get the People panel open by default.

- **Red means REC and nothing else.** The REC pill (take number and elapsed time, counted from the host's take start so a mid-take joiner agrees), a red rule under the top bar for the whole take, the red own-track waveform, the tab title (`REC take 3 - Castcorder`) and favicon, and the tray dot. Muted is an inverted button with a slashed mic, never red. Amber is for things to check; warnings say what still works and never block.

- **Mute is call-only.** It disables the live stream's microphone track; the raw microphone, which is the track, keeps recording. The first mute explains this once per browser profile, and the dock's own-track waveform keeps moving while muted.

- **Two signals per person, kept apart:** live (are we hearing them?) and track (is their machine recording?). Only signals backed by real data are drawn. Live: each remote tile has a green waveform and speaking ring from the audio actually arriving (an AnalyserNode on their received track, one shared animation loop at 15 Hz), so a flat line means nothing arrives; the host machine's own participant is metered the same way. Remote mute: a muted device keeps reporting its raw microphone level (level only, never audio, about 10 Hz, `MicState` on "control", relayed by the host to everyone else); the others see a slashed mic and a dimmed but moving waveform and no speaking ring, which says it is still recording. The host machine's own participant mutes the same way (call-only, a volume element before its encoder) and reports `MicState` through the host. Track: every device acknowledges each recorded source per take (`SourceAck` on "control": recording, lost, or unavailable; a new segment acknowledges again, and so does every rejoin mid-take); the host's own participant's recorder does the same through its device-loss watchdog. The host works out each participant's track chip (Ready while no take records; Starting from the take start until the microphone is acknowledged; Recording; Mic lost until a new segment records; Cannot record; and Cannot record video, from the existing cannot-meet-profile report, while the microphone records) and sends it (`Recorders`) to producers for every participant and to each device for itself, whenever it changes. Every tile shows its chip (top left; red only while recording, amber for Mic lost and Cannot record video), and producers' top bar shows "N of M recording" during a take (amber while short), counting microphones recording.

- **Icon.** A mic inside broadcast arcs on slate (`castcorder-app/icons/src`, `task app:icons`); state is a corner badge painted at runtime: red recording, amber needs a look (a device short of disk, the library volume included, a source that cannot meet the recording profile, or a microphone lost mid-take), blue while guests sync; red wins over amber, amber over blue.

- **Theme.** System, Dark or Light, a toggle in the lobby and the top bar, remembered per browser or desktop webview in localStorage (`castcorder.theme`). An explicit choice is `data-theme` on `<html>` and overrides `prefers-color-scheme`; System removes it. index.html applies it before first paint.

- **Call link.** The call link is always visible with a Copy button: the rendezvous link when there is one, else the LAN https link the host prints. The host machine's lobby shows it before joining (`GET /role` on the localhost listener carries `link` and `lan`; LAN guests are not handed it); a producer's top bar shows it in the call (the room's link, else the page's own LAN origin).

- **Waveform scale.** Levels map to dBFS (`castcorder-client/src/levels.rs`), drawn relative to an adaptive per-stream noise floor (falls fast to quieter readings, rises about 1 dB/s): flat up to 6 dB above it, full at 40 dB above or 0 dBFS. The dock and the tiles share it, and the speaking ring is 12 dB above the floor (6 to stop), so a noisy webcam mic idles flat and speech stands out.

- **Open.** The live transcript (cast-7hm).

## Devices

Each participant has one live source per kind and a set of recorded sources (cast-94c; castcorder-client `app/devices.rs`).

- **Picking.** The lobby has a Microphone, Camera and Speakers picker ("System default" first; Speakers only where the browser has `setSinkId`), remembered per browser profile (localStorage `castcorder.devices`: per kind the device id and name; a device whose id changed is found again by name, and one the browser does not list is kept as picked). A browser that names no device before one is allowed (Firefox) offers "Show device names", which opens the microphone and camera for a moment. That offer sits below Join and its space is kept from the first draw: the browser says whether it names devices only after the lobby is drawn, and the lobby centres its form, so a row appearing anywhere would move Join from under a click. With nothing picked the constraints are what they always were, and a picked device that is gone falls back to the default.
- **Live and recorded.** The picked microphone is the live source the others hear and also the main recorded source (`mic`); the picked camera is the live video. The in-call Devices dialog (dock button `#devices`) separates the two: Live (microphone, camera, speakers) and Recorded on this computer (the main track and any backup microphones). On the host machine's own page it also holds the native microphone panel.
- **Switching mid-call.** The new device is opened first, so one that is refused changes nothing. A microphone switch swaps the audio sender's track (`replaceTrack`: no renegotiation, mute kept); a camera switch reopens the camera on the same video sender. Mid-take a microphone switch ends the main track's segment and continues it in a new segment on the new device, exactly as after a dropout, but acknowledged as recording throughout (no Mic lost). Decided so because a different device can bring a different sample rate or channel count, which one WAV and one timing file cannot hold; each segment has one device's audio and its own timing metadata, and its sidecar names the device (`sourceLabel`), so the source id names the role (`mic`) and the sidecar names the device. A camera switch mid-take likewise ends that video track and starts a new one.
- **Backup microphones.** "Add backup microphone" records another input as its own recorded source (`mic-2`, `mic-3`, ...), never live: opened raw like the main microphone, through a storage worker of its own (`crate::worker` named `backup`: it reads its source and writes its track, writes no log files and never scans or offers; the storage worker offers its finished tracks like any other, so they land as `takeNN_<who>_mic-2.wav` with sidecar and timing). It acknowledges per take like the main microphone. Added mid-take it records from then on; removed mid-take its segment is closed first. Backups are per call: Leave closes and drops them.
- **Cannot record.** A backup that cannot be opened or recorded here (Firefox has no MediaStreamTrackProcessor and gets the AudioWorklet path, a device may be busy) stays listed as unavailable; the page warns that its main microphone still records, and at each take start (or at once mid-take) it acknowledges the source Unavailable and reports `ProfileUnmet` to producers. The take goes on.
- **Tested.** Unit: backup track names. Browser (`lib/devices.mjs`, Chromium, Chromium without page MediaStreamTrackProcessor (backups on the AudioWorklet path) and Firefox, a host of its own): lobby picks survive a reload (Firefox through "Show device names"); the dialog shows them and every participant's audio plays through the picked speakers; a backup added before the take records beside the main microphone; a mid-take microphone switch keeps the same connection, stays connected, X still hears Y, and the main track lands as two verified segments named for their devices in order, the second starting after the first ends, each on the host timeline; the backup lands as its own verified track with timing; each segment and the backup are aligned as tracks of their own; a backup whose device is refused is listed unavailable, warned and reported to producers; Leave releases every device.
- **Open.** Producers selecting and adding sources on any guest's device, and device names shown to producers (cast-swn). The host machine's native microphone switching mid-call and native backups (cast-1xd). In Chromium a picked microphone on an HTTPS page sometimes gives the page MediaStreamTrackProcessor no audio at join, so capture falls back to the AudioWorklet path (cast-nbq). The fake devices share one format, so segments of different formats are untested (each segment is aligned as a file of its own).
